“If we take you with us,” he said solemnly, “it can only be as believers in our faith. We won’t have wolves among our flock. It’s far better for your bones to bleach in this wilderness than for you to become that small spot of rot that eventually spoils the whole fruit. Will you come with us under those terms?”

“I’ll come with you under any terms,” said Ferrier, with such force that the serious Elders couldn’t help but smile. Only the leader kept his stern, commanding expression.

“Take him, Brother Stangerson,” he said. “Give him food and drink, and the child as well. It’s also your duty to teach him our holy faith. We’ve delayed long enough. Forward! On, on to Zion!”

“On, on to Zion!” shouted the crowd of Mormons. The cry ran down the long caravan, passed from mouth to mouth, and faded into a dull murmur in the distance. With the crack of whips and the creak of wheels, the great wagons began to move, and soon the whole caravan was on the road again. The Elder who had been given charge of the two strays led them to his wagon, where a meal was already waiting.

“You’ll stay here,” he said. “In a few days you’ll have recovered from your exhaustion. In the meantime, remember that from now on and forever you belong to our faith. Brigham Young has said it, and he has spoken with the voice of Joseph Smith, which is the voice of God.”
