Cutting off their protests, he stripped off his own clothes and donned the prisoner’s silk breeches and sandals, tying the band from the man’s hair around his own head but ignoring the short fisherman’s knife. Commoners in Stygia were forbidden to carry swords, and the cloak was too small to conceal Conan’s long blade. Instead, he fastened a Ghanata knife to his hip—a weapon favored by the fierce desert tribes south of Stygia. It was a broad, heavy, slightly curved blade of fine steel, razor-sharp and long enough to hew a man apart.

Then, leaving the Stygian guarded by the corsairs, Conan climbed into the fisherman’s boat.

“Wait for me until dawn,” he said. “If I haven’t returned by then, I never will. Head south to your homes.”

As he climbed over the rail, the men began a mournful wail at his departure, until he thrust his head back up to curse them into silence. Then, dropping into the boat, he seized the oars and sent the small craft skimming over the waves faster than its owner had ever managed.
