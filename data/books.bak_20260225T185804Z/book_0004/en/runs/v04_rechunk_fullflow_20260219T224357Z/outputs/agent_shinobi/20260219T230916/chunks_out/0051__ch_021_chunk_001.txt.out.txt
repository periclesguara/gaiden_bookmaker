“They are falling even now, far to the north,” answered Xaltotun. “I feel the flood rushing down the valley. Conan will never cross the river.”  

Amalric shuddered. He could not shake the feeling that he was dealing with something monstrous and unnatural.  

“Tomorrow we’ll march to Tanasul,” he said. “We’ll be ready when Tarascus joins us.”  

* * * * *  

The hosts of Amalric and Valerius marched eastward through the gray dawn, their armor glinting dully under the overcast sky. The wind blew cold from the north, carrying the smell of rain. The Shirki roared louder than usual, swollen by the unseen floods racing down from the mountains.  

By noon, the first drops began to fall, and by nightfall, the rain was a solid curtain, drumming on helmets and tents, turning the plain into a sea of mud. The river rose higher and higher, foaming and swirling, until the crossing at Tanasul was a raging torrent.  

Conan’s scouts, watching from the hills, saw the flood and cursed. The king himself stood on a rocky height, his black armor streaked with rain, his dark face grim. He had planned to cross the river at dawn, join the Gundermen, and crush the Nemedians between the two armies. Now the swollen river barred his way.  

He turned to his captains.  

“The gods are against us today,” he said. “We’ll camp here and wait for the waters to fall. But keep the men ready. Amalric will come.”  

And Amalric came.  

Through the driving rain, his army slogged forward, the drums of Nemedia beating a dull, ominous rhythm. The plain shook under the tramp of thousands of feet. The lightning flashed on helmets and spearheads, and the thunder rolled like the voice of doom.  

In the gray dawn that followed, the two armies faced each other across the valley of the Shirki. Between them lay a sea of mud and the roaring river. Behind Conan rose the dark hills, and behind Amalric stretched the plain toward Tanasul.  

The drums of peril beat louder.
