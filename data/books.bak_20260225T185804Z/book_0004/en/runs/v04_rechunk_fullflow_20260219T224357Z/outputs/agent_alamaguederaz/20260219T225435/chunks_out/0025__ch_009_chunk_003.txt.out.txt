Cursing in surprise, Conan scooped up Albiona with one powerful arm and followed the stranger who had come to his aid. He wasn’t about to hesitate—not with thirty prison guards closing in behind him.  

Surrounded by the mysterious figures, he hurried down the alley, carrying the countess as if she were a child. He couldn’t tell anything about his rescuers except that they wore dark cloaks and hoods. Doubt and suspicion flickered through his mind, but since they had struck down his enemies, he saw no better option than to follow them.  

As if sensing his hesitation, the leader lightly touched his arm and said, “Don’t be afraid, King Conan; we are your loyal subjects.” The voice was unfamiliar, but the accent was Aquilonian, from the central provinces.  

Behind them, the guards shouted as they stumbled over the bodies lying in the mud, then came charging furiously down the alley, seeing only a vague dark shape moving between them and the light of the distant street. But the hooded men suddenly turned toward what looked like a solid wall, and Conan saw a door open there. He muttered a curse. He had walked that alley many times in daylight and had never noticed a door there. But through it they went, and the door closed behind them with the click of a lock. The sound didn’t make him feel any safer, but his guides urged him forward, moving with practiced precision, steering Conan with a hand on each elbow. It felt like passing through a tunnel, and Conan felt Albiona’s slender body trembling in his arms. Then, somewhere ahead, an opening became faintly visible—a slightly lighter arch in the darkness—and through it they passed.  

After that came a confusing series of dim courtyards, shadowy alleys, and twisting corridors, all crossed in complete silence, until at last they entered a wide, brightly lit chamber. Conan couldn’t even guess where they were, for their winding route had completely defeated even his barbarian sense of direction.
