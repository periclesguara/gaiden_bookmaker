Cutting off their protests, he took off his own clothes and put on the prisoner’s silk pants and sandals, along with the band from the man’s hair, but ignored the short fisherman’s knife. Ordinary people in Stygia weren’t allowed to carry swords, and the cloak wasn’t big enough to hide Conan’s long blade. Instead, he strapped a Ghanata knife to his hip—a weapon used by the fierce desert tribes south of Stygia. It was a broad, heavy, slightly curved blade made of fine steel, razor-sharp and long enough to cut a man in half.

Then, leaving the Stygian guarded by the corsairs, Conan climbed into the fisherman’s boat.

“Wait for me until dawn,” he said. “If I haven’t come back by then, I never will. Head south to your homes.”

As he climbed over the rail, the men began a sorrowful wail at his leaving, until he stuck his head back up to curse them into silence. Then, dropping into the boat, he grabbed the oars and drove the small craft speeding over the waves faster than its owner had ever done.
