“I’ll ride to Poitain if I can,” Conan said finally. “But I’ll go alone. And I still have one last duty to carry out as the king of Aquilonia.”  

“What do you mean, Your Majesty?” asked Servius, his voice trembling with fear.  

“I’m going into Tarantia tonight for Albiona,” the king replied. “It seems I’ve failed all my other loyal subjects—if they take her head, they might as well take mine too.”  

“This is insanity!” shouted Servius, jumping to his feet and grabbing his throat as if he could already feel the rope tightening around it.  

“There are things about the Tower that only a few people know,” said Conan. “Either way, I’d be a coward to let Albiona die for staying true to me. I might be a king without a kingdom, but I’m not a man without honor.”  

“It will destroy us all!” Servius whispered.  

“It will destroy no one but me if I fail. You’ve already risked enough. I’m riding alone tonight. All I need from you is an eye patch, a walking stick, and clothes like those worn by travelers.”
