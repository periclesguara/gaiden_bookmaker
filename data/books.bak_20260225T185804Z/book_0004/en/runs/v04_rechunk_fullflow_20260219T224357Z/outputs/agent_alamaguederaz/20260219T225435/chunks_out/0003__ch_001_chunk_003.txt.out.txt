It was strange and unsettling, but everyone watching understood that it was only the visible reflection of Orastes’ thoughts, shown in the mirror the same way a wizard’s thoughts appear in a magic crystal. The image drifted in a blur for a moment, then suddenly came into sharp focus—a tall man, broad-shouldered and deep-chested, with a thick, powerful neck and heavily muscled arms and legs. He was dressed in silk and velvet, with the golden lions of Aquilonia embroidered on his fine tunic, and the crown of Aquilonia shone on his square-cut black hair. Yet the great sword at his side seemed to suit him more naturally than the royal clothing. His forehead was wide and low, his eyes a fiery blue that seemed to burn from within. His dark, scarred, almost grim face was that of a warrior, and even the velvet clothes couldn’t hide the hard, dangerous strength of his body.

“That man isn’t a Hyborian!” exclaimed Xaltotun.

“No; he’s a Cimmerian, one of those wild tribesmen who live in the gray northern hills.”

“I fought his ancestors long ago,” muttered Xaltotun. “Not even the kings of Acheron could conquer them.”

“They’re still feared by the southern nations,” said Orastes. “He’s a true son of that fierce race, and so far, no one has been able to defeat him.”

Xaltotun didn’t reply; he sat staring down at the pool of living fire that shimmered in his hand. Outside, the hound howled again, long and chilling.
