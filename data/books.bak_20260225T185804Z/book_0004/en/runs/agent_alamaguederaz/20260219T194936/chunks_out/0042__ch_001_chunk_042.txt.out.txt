Suddenly, a door opened in the wall ahead of him, and he slipped into the shadow of an archway. A figure stepped out of the open door and moved down the alley—not sneaking, but moving with a natural, silent grace, like a jungle predator. Enough starlight filtered into the narrow passage to outline the man’s profile faintly as he passed the doorway where Conan hid. The stranger was a Stygian. There was no mistaking that hawk-like face and shaven head, even in the dim light, nor the cloak draped over his broad shoulders. He continued down the alley toward the beach, and for a moment Conan thought the man might be carrying a lantern hidden in his clothes, for he caught a brief flash of glowing light just as the figure disappeared.

But Conan quickly forgot the stranger when he noticed that the door the man had come through was still open. Conan had planned to enter through the main entrance and force Servio to show him the room where the Zingaran slept. But if he could get inside without drawing attention, all the better.

A few long strides brought him to the door, and as his hand touched the lock, he stifled a grunt of surprise. His skilled fingers—trained long ago among the thieves of Zamora—told him that the lock had been broken, apparently by some tremendous force from the outside that had twisted and bent the heavy iron bolts, tearing the sockets loose from the doorframe. How such damage could have been done so violently without waking the whole neighborhood was beyond him, but he was sure it had happened that very night. A broken lock, if discovered, would never go unrepaired in Servio’s house, not in this district of thieves and killers.

Conan slipped inside quietly, dagger in hand, wondering how he would find the Zingaran’s room. Feeling his way through total darkness, he suddenly stopped. He sensed death in the air, the way a wild animal does—not as a threat to himself, but as the presence of something freshly killed. In the dark, his foot struck something heavy and soft. With a sudden chill of foreboding, he groped along the wall until he found the shelf that held a brass lamp, with flint, steel, and tinder beside it. A few seconds later, a flickering, uncertain light sprang up, and he looked around sharply.

A bunk built against the rough stone wall, a bare table, and a bench were the only furnishings in the shabby room. An inner door stood closed and bolted. On the hard-packed dirt floor lay Beloso. He was on his back, his head pulled back between his shoulders so that his wide, glassy eyes seemed to stare at the soot-stained beams of the cobwebbed ceiling. His lips were drawn back from his teeth in a frozen grimace of agony. His sword lay near him, still sheathed. His shirt was torn open, and on his brown, muscular chest was the imprint of a black hand—thumb and four fingers clearly visible.

Conan stared in silence, the hair on the back of his neck bristling.

“Crom,” he muttered. “The black hand of Set!”

He had seen that mark before—the death sign of the black priests of Set, the grim cult that ruled in shadowed Stygia. And suddenly he remembered the strange flash of light he had seen coming from the mysterious Stygian who had stepped out of this very room.

“The Heart, by Crom!” he muttered. “He was carrying it under his cloak. He stole it. He blasted that door open with his sorcery and killed Beloso. He was a priest of Set.”

A quick search confirmed at least part of his suspicion. The jewel was gone from the Zingaran’s body. A growing unease stirred in Conan—a sense that this was no random act, no coincidence. He was convinced that the mysterious Stygian galley had come into Messantia’s harbor for a specific purpose. How could the priests of Set have known that the Heart had traveled south? Yet the thought was no more unbelievable than the dark magic that could kill an armed man with the touch of an open, empty hand.

A quiet footstep outside the door made him spin around like a great cat. In one motion he snuffed out the lamp and drew his sword. His ears told him that men were out there in the darkness, closing in on the doorway. As his eyes adjusted to the sudden blackness, he could make out faint shapes surrounding the entrance. He couldn’t guess who they were, but as always, he acted first—leaping suddenly out of the doorway before they could strike.

His unexpected move caught them completely off guard. He sensed and heard men all around him, saw a dim, masked figure in the starlight before him—then his sword drove home, and he was sprinting down the alley before the slower-thinking attackers could react.

As he ran, he heard ahead of him the faint creak of oarlocks, and he forgot the men behind him. A boat was moving out into the bay! Gritting his teeth, he pushed himself faster, but before he reached the beach, he heard the rasp and creak of ropes and the deep grind of a great sweep turning in its socket.

Thick clouds rolling in from the sea blotted out the stars. In near-total darkness, Conan reached the shore, straining his eyes toward the restless black water. Something was moving out there—a long, low, dark shape gliding away, gathering speed as it went. To his ears came the rhythmic clack of long oars. He ground his teeth in helpless rage. It was the Stygian galley, racing out to sea, carrying with it the jewel that meant the throne of Aquilonia to him.

With a savage curse, he stepped toward the waves lapping at the sand, grabbing at his mail shirt, ready to tear it off and swim after the fleeing ship. Then the crunch of a heel in the sand behind him made him whirl around. He had forgotten his pursuers.

Dark figures rushed at him through the sand. The first went down under the Cimmerian’s sweeping sword, but the others didn’t hesitate. Blades flashed dimly in the darkness or scraped against his armor. Blood and entrails splattered his hand, and someone screamed as he slashed upward with deadly force. A muttered voice urged the attackers on, and that voice sounded faintly familiar. Conan hacked his way through the clutching, striking figures toward the sound. A faint gleam of light through the drifting clouds revealed a tall, gaunt man with a large, pale scar on his temple. Conan’s sword split his skull as easily as slicing through a ripe melon.
