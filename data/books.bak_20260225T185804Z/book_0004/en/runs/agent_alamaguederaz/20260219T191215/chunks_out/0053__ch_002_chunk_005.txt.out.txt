And now he knew his ears weren’t deceiving him. He was being followed, and whoever or whatever was after him was getting closer. In the darkness came shuffling and sliding sounds that no human feet could have made—nor the feet of any normal animal. Maybe the underworld had its own monstrous life. They were behind him. He turned to face them, though he couldn’t see a thing, and slowly backed away. Then the sounds stopped, even before he turned his head and saw, somewhere down the long corridor, a faint glow of light.

19 - IN THE HALL OF THE DEAD  
In the Hall of the Dead  

Conan moved carefully toward the light he had seen, listening intently behind him, but there was no further sound of pursuit, though he could feel the darkness alive with awareness.  

The glow wasn’t still; it moved, bobbing eerily along. Then he saw what caused it. The tunnel he was in crossed another, wider corridor some distance ahead. Along that corridor moved a strange procession—four tall, thin men in black hooded robes, leaning on staffs. The leader held a torch above his head—a torch that burned with a steady, unnatural light. Like ghosts they passed across his limited view and vanished, leaving only a fading glow to mark their passing. Their appearance was indescribably uncanny. They weren’t Stygians, nor like anything Conan had ever seen. He doubted they were even human. They looked like black phantoms, stalking like ghouls through the haunted tunnels.  

But his situation couldn’t be any worse than it already was. Before the inhuman feet behind him could start slithering forward again when the distant light faded, Conan was running down the corridor. He plunged into the other tunnel and saw, far ahead, small and distant, the strange procession moving within the glowing sphere. He crept silently after them, then suddenly pressed himself against the wall as he saw them stop and gather together as if discussing something. They turned as if to go back, and he slipped into the nearest archway. Feeling his way in the darkness—darkness he had grown so used to that he could almost see through it—he found that the tunnel curved rather than ran straight, and he moved back beyond the first bend so the strangers’ light wouldn’t fall on him as they passed.  

But as he stood there, he became aware of a low hum of sound from somewhere behind him, like the murmur of human voices. Moving down the corridor toward it, he confirmed what he had first suspected. Giving up his plan to follow the ghostly travelers to whatever destination they sought, he turned toward the voices instead.  

Soon he saw a glimmer of light ahead, and turning into the corridor from which it came, he saw a wide arch filled with a dim glow at the far end. On his left, a narrow stone staircase led upward, and instinctively cautious, he turned and climbed the stairs. The voices he heard came from beyond that glowing arch.  

The sounds faded below him as he climbed, and soon he emerged through a low arched doorway into a vast open space glowing with an eerie light.  

He was standing on a shadowy balcony looking down into a broad, dimly lit hall of enormous size. It was a hall of the dead, seen only by the silent priests of Stygia. Along the black walls rose tier upon tier of carved and painted sarcophagi. Each stood in a niche in the dark stone, and the tiers climbed higher and higher until they were lost in the shadows above. Thousands of carved faces stared down impassively at the group in the center of the hall, making them seem small and meaningless in the presence of so many dead.  

There were ten priests in the group, and though they had removed their masks, Conan recognized them as the same priests he had accompanied to the pyramid. They stood before a tall, hawk-faced man beside a black altar on which lay a mummy wrapped in rotting bandages. The altar seemed to stand in the heart of a living fire that pulsed and shimmered, dripping flakes of trembling golden flame onto the black stones around it. This dazzling light came from a great red jewel lying on the altar, and in its reflection the priests’ faces looked pale and corpse-like. As he watched, Conan felt the weight of all the long miles and weary days and nights of his quest pressing down on him, and he trembled with the wild urge to rush among those silent priests, cut his way through with mighty blows of his sword, and seize the red gem with desperate hands. But he forced himself to stay still, crouching in the shadow of the stone railing. A quick glance showed him that a stairway led down from the balcony into the hall, hugging the wall and half hidden in shadow. He peered into the dimness of the vast chamber, searching for other priests or worshipers, but saw only the group around the altar.  

In that great emptiness, the voice of the man beside the altar sounded hollow and ghostly:  

“…And so the word came southward. The night wind whispered it, the ravens croaked it as they flew, and the grim bats told it to the owls and the serpents that hide in ancient ruins. Werewolves and vampires knew, and the black-bodied demons that prowl by night. The sleeping Night of the World stirred and shook its heavy mane, and there began a beating of drums in deep darkness, and the echoes of strange cries frightened men who walked at dusk. For the Heart of Ahriman had come again into the world to fulfill its mysterious destiny.  

“Ask me not how I, Thutothmes of Khemi and the Night, heard the word before Thoth-Amon, who calls himself prince of all wizards. There are secrets not meant for ears even such as yours, and Thoth-Amon is not the only master of the Black Ring.  

“I knew, and I went to meet the Heart as it came southward. It was like a magnet drawing me without fail. From death to death it came, riding on a river of human blood. Blood feeds it, blood calls to it. Its power is greatest when there is blood on the hands that hold it, when it is taken by slaughter from its owner. Wherever it shines, blood is spilled and kingdoms fall, and the forces of nature are thrown into chaos.  

“And here I stand, master of the Heart, and I have summoned you secretly, my faithful ones, to share in the dark kingdom that is to come. Tonight you shall witness the breaking of Thoth-Amon’s chains that enslave us, and the birth of an empire.”
