The barons who had followed you faithfully would never follow one of their own, since each thought himself as good as the next and feared the ambitions of the others. You were the cord that held the bundle of sticks together. When that cord was cut, the bundle fell apart. If you had had a son, the barons would have rallied to him with loyalty. But there was no one for their patriotism to center on.

The merchants and commoners, terrified of chaos and a return to the feudal days when every baron ruled by his own law, cried out that any king was better than none—even Valerius, who at least carried the blood of the old dynasty. No one stood against him when he rode up at the head of his armored hosts, the scarlet dragon of Nemedia flying above him, and struck his lance against the gates of Tarantia.

No, the people opened the gates and knelt in the dust before him. They had refused to help Prospero defend the city. They said they would rather be ruled by Valerius than by Trocero. They said—truthfully—that the barons would not rally to Trocero, but many would accept Valerius. They claimed that by surrendering to Valerius they would avoid the destruction of civil war and the wrath of the Nemedians. Prospero rode south with his ten thousand knights, and the Nemedian horsemen entered the city a few hours later. They did not follow him. They stayed to see Valerius crowned in Tarantia.

“Then the old witch’s smoke told the truth,” muttered Conan, feeling a strange chill crawl down his spine. “Amalric crowned Valerius?”

“Yes, in the coronation hall, with the blood of slaughter barely dried on his hands.”

“And do the people prosper under his kind rule?” asked Conan with bitter sarcasm.

“He lives like a foreign prince in the heart of a conquered land,” answered Servius grimly. “His court is filled with Nemedians, his palace guards are of the same breed, and a great garrison of them occupies the citadel. Yes, the Hour of the Dragon has truly come.

“Nemedians strut like lords through the streets. Women are violated, merchants robbed daily, and Valerius either cannot or will not restrain them. No, he is only their puppet, their figurehead. The wise knew he would be, and now the people are beginning to see it too.

“Amalric has marched out with a strong army to subdue the outlying provinces where some barons still defy him. But there is no unity among them. Their jealousy of one another is stronger than their fear of Amalric. He will crush them one by one. Many castles and cities, realizing this, have already surrendered. Those who resist suffer terribly. The Nemedians are gorging themselves on their long-held hatred. Their ranks are swelled by Aquilonians whom fear, greed, or the need for survival have driven into their armies. It’s the natural result.”

Conan nodded darkly, staring at the red firelight flickering across the richly carved oak panels.

“Aquilonia has a king instead of the anarchy they feared,” said Servius at last. “But Valerius does not protect his people from his allies. Hundreds who could not pay the ransom demanded of them have been sold to the Kothic slave traders.”

Conan’s head snapped up, and a deadly fire blazed in his blue eyes. He cursed fiercely, his massive hands clenching into iron fists.

“Yes, white men sell white men and white women, just as in the old feudal days. In the palaces of Shem and Turan they will live out their lives as slaves. Valerius is king, but the unity the people hoped for, even if forged by the sword, is still incomplete.

“Gunderland in the north and Poitain in the south remain unconquered, and there are still free provinces in the west, where the border barons are supported by the Bossonian bowmen. Yet these distant provinces pose no real threat to Valerius. They must stay on the defensive and will be lucky if they can keep their independence. Here, Valerius and his foreign knights rule supreme.”

“Then let him enjoy it while he can,” said Conan grimly. “His time is short. The people will rise when they learn I’m alive. We’ll retake Tarantia before Amalric can return with his army. Then we’ll drive these dogs out of the kingdom.”

Servius said nothing. The crackle of the fire filled the silence.

“Well,” demanded Conan impatiently, “why do you sit there with your head bowed, staring at the hearth? Do you doubt my words?”

Servius avoided the king’s gaze.

“What any mortal man can do, you will do, Your Majesty,” he said. “I’ve ridden behind you in battle, and I know no living man can stand before your sword.”

“Then what is it?”

Servius pulled his fur-lined tunic tighter around him and shivered despite the fire.

“Men say your downfall was caused by sorcery,” he said after a moment.

“So what?”

“What mortal can fight against sorcery? Who is this veiled man who, as they say, meets with Valerius and his allies at midnight, appearing and vanishing so mysteriously? Men whisper that he is a great magician who died thousands of years ago but has returned from the gray lands of death to overthrow the king of Aquilonia and restore the dynasty from which Valerius descends.”

“What of it?” Conan burst out angrily. “I escaped from the devil-ridden pits of Belverus and from dark magic in the mountains. If the people rise—”

Servius shook his head.

“Your strongest supporters in the eastern and central provinces are dead, fled, or imprisoned. Gunderland lies far to the north, Poitain far to the south. The Bossonians have withdrawn to their western marches. It would take weeks to gather and unite those forces, and before that could happen, Amalric would attack each levy separately and destroy them.”

“But an uprising in the central provinces would turn the tide for us!” exclaimed Conan. “We could seize Tarantia and hold it against Amalric until the Gundermen and Poitanians arrived.”

Servius hesitated, his voice dropping to a whisper.

“Men say you died under a curse. They say this veiled stranger cast a spell on you to kill you and break your army. The great bell has already tolled your funeral. People believe you are dead. And the central provinces would not rise, even if they knew you lived. They would not dare. Sorcery defeated you at Valkia. Sorcery carried the news to Tarantia, for that very night men were shouting it in the streets.”
