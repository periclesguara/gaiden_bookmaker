"I’ve seen things that have shattered my soul. In the heart of the sleeping hills, I’ve watched Xaltotun speak with the souls of the damned and summon the ancient demons of forgotten Acheron. I’ve seen the cursed descendants of that cursed empire worship him and hail him as their high priest. I’ve seen what he plans—and I tell you, it’s nothing less than the resurrection of the ancient, dark, terrible kingdom of Acheron!"

"What are you saying?" demanded Amalric. "Acheron is dust. There aren’t enough survivors left to form an empire. Not even Xaltotun can rebuild the dust of three thousand years."

"You know little of his dark powers," answered Orastes grimly. "I’ve seen the very hills take on a strange, ancient look under the spell of his incantations. I’ve glimpsed, like shadows behind reality, the faint shapes and outlines of valleys, forests, mountains, and lakes—not as they are now, but as they were in that dim past—and I’ve even sensed, more than seen, the purple towers of forgotten Python shimmering like misty figures in the twilight.

"And in the last gathering I attended with him, I finally understood his sorcery, while the drums thundered and the beast-like worshippers howled with their faces in the dust. I tell you, he intends to restore Acheron through his magic—through a blood sacrifice so vast the world has never seen its equal. He would enslave the world and, with a flood of blood, wash away the present and bring back the past!"

"You’re insane!" exclaimed Tarascus.

"Insane?" Orastes turned a haunted stare on him. "Can any man see what I’ve seen and stay completely sane? Yet I speak the truth. He plans the return of Acheron, with its towers, wizards, kings, and horrors, just as it was long ago. The descendants of Acheron will serve as the foundation on which he’ll rebuild, but it’s the blood and bodies of the people of today that will provide the mortar and stones for that rebuilding. I can’t tell you how. My mind reels when I try to understand. But I have seen! Acheron will be Acheron again, and even the hills, forests, and rivers will take back their ancient forms. Why not? If I, with my small knowledge, could bring back to life a man dead three thousand years, why couldn’t the greatest wizard in the world bring back to life a kingdom dead three thousand years? From the dust, Acheron will rise again at his command."

"How can we stop him?" asked Tarascus, shaken.

"There’s only one way," answered Orastes. "We must steal the Heart of Ahriman!"

"But I—" began Tarascus involuntarily, then quickly shut his mouth.

No one noticed him, and Orastes went on.

"It’s a power that can be used against him. With it in my hands, I might defy him. But how can we steal it? He’s hidden it in some secret place, one even a Zamorian thief couldn’t find. I can’t discover where it’s hidden. If only he would sleep again under the black lotus—but the last time he did was after the battle of Valkia, when he was exhausted from the great magic he had worked, and—"

The door, though locked and bolted, swung silently open, and Xaltotun stood before them—calm, composed, stroking his long beard—but the flickering lights of hell burned in his eyes.

"I’ve taught you too much," he said calmly, pointing a finger like a weapon of doom at Orastes. Before anyone could move, he threw a handful of dust on the floor near the priest’s feet. Orastes stood frozen like a statue. The dust flared and smoldered; a thin blue smoke rose and coiled upward around Orastes in a slender spiral. When it reached above his shoulders, it whipped around his neck with the speed of a striking snake. Orastes’ scream was cut off in a choking gurgle. His hands flew to his throat, his eyes bulged, his tongue protruded. The smoke looked like a blue rope tightening around his neck; then it faded and vanished, and Orastes collapsed to the floor, dead.

Xaltotun clapped his hands, and two men entered—men often seen with him—small, vile-looking, dark-skinned, with red slanted eyes and sharp, rat-like teeth. They said nothing. Lifting the corpse, they carried it away.

With a dismissive wave of his hand, Xaltotun sat down at the ivory table where the pale kings were gathered.

"Why are you meeting in secret?" he demanded.

"The Aquilonians have risen in the west," answered Amalric, recovering from the shock of Orastes’ death. "The fools believe Conan is alive and coming at the head of a Poitanian army to reclaim his kingdom. If he had reappeared right after Valkia, or if a rumor had spread that he lived, the central provinces wouldn’t have dared rise against us—they feared your powers too much. But they’ve grown so desperate under Valerius’ misrule that they’ll follow any man who can unite them against us, preferring sudden death to endless torture and misery.

"Of course, the rumor has long persisted that Conan wasn’t really killed at Valkia, but only recently have the masses begun to believe it. Pallantides has returned from exile in Ophir, swearing that the king was sick in his tent that day, and that a man-at-arms wore his armor. A squire who only lately recovered from a mace blow at Valkia confirms his story—or pretends to.

"An old woman with a pet wolf has wandered across the land, proclaiming that King Conan still lives and will return one day to reclaim his crown. And lately, the cursed priests of Asura are preaching the same thing. They claim they’ve received word by some mysterious means that Conan is returning to reconquer his realm. I can’t catch her or them. It’s obviously one of Trocero’s tricks. My spies tell me there’s undeniable proof that the Poitanians are gathering to invade Aquilonia. I believe Trocero will present some impostor and claim he’s King Conan."

Tarascus laughed, but there was no real conviction in his laughter. He secretly touched a scar beneath his tunic and remembered the ravens that had followed a fugitive’s trail; remembered the body of his squire, Arideus, brought back from the border mountains horribly mangled—by a great gray wolf, his terrified soldiers had said. But he also remembered a red jewel stolen from a golden chest while a wizard slept, and he said nothing.
