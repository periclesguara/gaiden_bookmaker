Cursing, the king hacked to the right and left with his broadsword, stabbing and slashing with his dagger. Blood-soaked fangs gleamed in the moonlight, filthy claws reached for him, but he cut his way through to the stallion, grabbed the reins, and leaped into the saddle. His sword rose and fell, a cold arc in the moonlight, spraying blood as it split deformed heads and hacked through staggering bodies. The stallion reared, biting and kicking. They broke through and thundered down the road. For a short distance on either side, gray, hideous shadows flickered. Then those fell behind, and Conan, reaching a wooded ridge, saw a vast stretch of bare slopes rising and rolling away before him.

13 - "A GHOST OUT OF THE PAST"  
"A Ghost Out of the Past"

Shortly after sunrise, Conan crossed the Argossean border. He had seen no sign of Beloso. Either the captain had escaped while the king lay unconscious, or he had fallen victim to the grim man-eaters of the Zingaran forest. But Conan had seen nothing to suggest the latter. The fact that he had lain undisturbed for so long seemed to show that the monsters had been busy chasing the captain instead. And if the man still lived, Conan was sure he was somewhere ahead on the road. Unless Beloso had planned to go into Argos, he would never have taken the eastward road to begin with.

The helmeted guards at the border didn’t question the Cimmerian. A lone wandering mercenary needed no passport or safe-conduct, especially when his plain armor showed he served no lord. Through the low grassy hills, where streams murmured and oak groves cast shifting light and shadow on the turf, he rode, following the long road that rose and dipped ahead of him over valleys and hills fading into the blue distance. It was an ancient road, this highway from Poitain to the sea.

Argos was at peace; heavy ox-carts rumbled along the road, and men with bare, sun-browned, muscular arms worked in orchards and fields that smiled beneath the branches of roadside trees. Old men sitting on benches before inns under spreading oaks called greetings to the traveler.

From the field workers, from the talkative old men in the inns where he quenched his thirst with great leather mugs of foaming ale, and from the sharp-eyed, silk-clad merchants he met on the road, Conan sought news of Beloso.

The stories conflicted, but Conan learned this much: a lean, wiry Zingaran with the dangerous black eyes and mustache typical of westerners was somewhere ahead on the road, apparently heading for Messantia. It was a logical destination; all the seaports of Argos were cosmopolitan, in sharp contrast to the inland provinces, and Messantia was the most diverse of them all. Ships from every maritime nation filled its harbor, and refugees and fugitives from many lands gathered there. The laws were loose; Messantia thrived on sea trade, and its citizens found it profitable to turn a blind eye when dealing with sailors. Not only legitimate trade flowed into Messantia; smugglers and pirates had their share in it too. Conan knew all this well, for in the old days, when he had been a Barachan pirate, he had sailed by night into Messantia’s harbor to unload strange cargoes. Most of the pirates of the Barachan Isles—small islands off Zingara’s southwestern coast—were Argossean sailors, and as long as they preyed only on the ships of other nations, the Argossean authorities were not too strict in enforcing maritime law.

But Conan had not limited himself to the Barachans’ activities. He had also sailed with the Zingaran buccaneers, and even with those wild black corsairs who swept up from the far south to raid the northern coasts, and that put him beyond the reach of any law. If he were recognized in any Argossean port, it would cost him his head. Yet he rode on toward Messantia without hesitation, stopping day or night only to rest his stallion and snatch a few moments of sleep for himself.

He entered the city without question, blending into the crowds that constantly flowed in and out of this great commercial hub. Messantia had no walls. The sea and its ships guarded the great southern trading city.

It was evening when Conan rode leisurely through the streets that sloped down toward the waterfront. At the ends of those streets he saw the wharves, the masts, and the sails of ships. He smelled salt water for the first time in years, heard the hum of rigging and the creak of spars in the breeze that was whipping up whitecaps beyond the headlands. Once again, the pull of faraway wandering stirred in his heart.

But he didn’t go down to the wharves. He turned aside and rode up a steep flight of wide, worn stone steps to a broad street where ornate white mansions overlooked the waterfront and harbor below. Here lived the men who had grown rich from the hard-earned wealth of the seas—some old sea captains who had found treasure abroad, but mostly traders and merchants who had never set foot on a bare deck or heard the roar of a storm or sea battle.

Conan turned his horse in through a certain gold-trimmed gate and rode into a courtyard where a fountain tinkled and pigeons fluttered from marble ledges to marble paving. A page in a jagged silk tunic and hose came forward, curious. The merchants of Messantia dealt with many strange and rough men, but most of them had the look of seafarers. It was unusual for a mercenary soldier to ride so freely into the courtyard of a wealthy merchant.

“The merchant Publio lives here?” Conan said—it was more a statement than a question, and something in his voice made the page remove his feathered cap as he bowed and replied, “Yes, my captain, he does.”

Conan dismounted, and the page called a servant, who came running to take the stallion’s reins.

“Your master is inside?” Conan asked, pulling off his gauntlets and brushing the road dust from his cloak and armor.

“Yes, my captain. Whom shall I announce?”

“I’ll announce myself,” Conan grunted. “I know the way well enough. Stay here.”

Obeying the sharp command, the page stood still, staring after Conan as he climbed a short flight of marble steps, wondering what connection his master could have with this giant warrior who looked every bit the northern barbarian.
