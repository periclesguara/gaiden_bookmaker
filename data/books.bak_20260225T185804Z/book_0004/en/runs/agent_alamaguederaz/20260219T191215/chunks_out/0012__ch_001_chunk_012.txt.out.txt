At last they reached the bottom of the stairs, then walked down a long, straight hallway. On one side was a blank wall broken only by the occasional arched doorway, each with a stairway leading upward behind it. On the other side was another wall lined with heavy barred doors spaced evenly every few feet.  

They stopped in front of one of these doors. One of the black-skinned men took a key from his belt and turned it in the lock. Pushing open the grille, they entered with their prisoner. The room was a small dungeon with thick stone walls, floor, and ceiling. In the opposite wall was another barred door. Conan couldn’t tell what lay beyond it, but he doubted it was another hallway. The flickering torchlight shining through the bars hinted at a large, shadowy space beyond, echoing and deep.  

In one corner of the dungeon, near the door they had entered through, a cluster of rusty chains hung from a huge iron ring set into the stone. A skeleton dangled from those chains. Conan stared at it with grim curiosity, noticing the condition of the bare bones—most were splintered and broken. The skull, which had fallen from the spine, was crushed as if by a savage blow of enormous strength.  

One of the black men, not the one who had opened the door, silently unlocked the chains from the ring with his key and dragged the heap of rusted metal and shattered bones aside. Then they fastened Conan’s chains to that same ring. The third man turned his key in the lock of the far door, grunting once he was sure it was secure.  

Then they looked at Conan with unreadable expressions, their slit eyes gleaming, the torchlight glinting off their dark, glossy skin.  

The one holding the key to the nearer door spoke in a rough, guttural voice: “This your palace now, white dog-king! Only master and we know. All palace sleep. We keep secret. You live and die here, maybe. Like him!” He kicked the broken skull contemptuously, sending it clattering across the stone floor.  

Conan didn’t bother to answer the insult. The man, angered by his silence, muttered a curse, bent down, and spat full in the king’s face. That was a mistake. Conan sat on the floor, chained by the waist, ankles, and wrists to the ring in the wall. He couldn’t stand or move more than a yard from the wall, but there was enough slack in the chains on his wrists. Before the man could pull his bullet-shaped head out of reach, Conan gathered the slack in one powerful hand and struck him on the head. The man dropped like a slaughtered ox, and his companions stared at him lying there, his scalp split open, blood seeping from his nose and ears.  

They made no move to retaliate, nor did they accept Conan’s fierce invitation to come within reach of the bloody chain in his hand. Grunting in their ape-like language, they finally lifted the unconscious man and carried him out like a sack of grain, his arms and legs dangling. They used his key to lock the door behind them but didn’t remove it from the gold chain attached to his belt. They took the torch with them, and as they moved down the corridor, the darkness crept in behind them like a living thing. Their soft, padded footsteps faded away along with the torchlight, leaving only silence and darkness.  

Conan lay still, enduring the weight of his chains and the hopelessness of his situation with the stoic toughness of the wild lands that had shaped him. He didn’t move, because the clanking of his chains sounded unnaturally loud in the stillness, and instinct—born from countless generations of wilderness ancestors—told him not to reveal his position while helpless. It wasn’t a conscious decision; he didn’t stay quiet because he reasoned that danger might be lurking in the dark. Xaltotun had told him he wouldn’t be harmed, and Conan believed it was in the man’s interest to keep him alive, at least for now. But the instincts of the wild were still in him, the same instincts that had made him lie silent as a child while beasts prowled near his hiding place.  

Even his sharp eyes couldn’t pierce the total darkness. But after a while—he couldn’t tell how long—a faint light appeared, a slanted gray beam by which he could just make out the bars of the door beside him and even the skeleton hanging from the other grille. This puzzled him until he realized the reason. He was deep underground, in the pits beneath the palace, but for some reason a shaft had been built from somewhere above. The moon had risen outside, and its light was shining faintly down the shaft. He realized he could use that light to tell the passing of days and nights. Maybe the sun would shine down it too, though it might be closed during the day. Perhaps it was a cruel trick, allowing a prisoner only a glimpse of daylight or moonlight.  

His eyes fell on the broken bones in the far corner, dimly glimmering. He didn’t waste thought wondering who the poor soul had been or why he had died there, but he did wonder at the shattered state of the bones. They hadn’t been broken on a torture rack. Then he noticed something else disturbing—the shinbones were split lengthwise. There was only one reason for that: someone had broken them open to get at the marrow. But what creature other than a human breaks bones for marrow? Maybe those remains were silent proof of a horrible act of cannibalism, some prisoner driven mad by starvation. Conan wondered if his own bones would one day hang there, rusted chains clinking around them. He fought down the wild panic of a trapped beast.  

The Cimmerian didn’t curse, scream, or weep like a civilized man might have. But the turmoil inside him was no less fierce. His great muscles trembled with the force of his emotions. Somewhere far to the west, the Nemedian army was cutting and burning its way through the heart of his kingdom. The small Poitanian force couldn’t stand against them. Prospero might hold Tarantia for weeks or even months, but eventually, without reinforcements, he would have to surrender to superior numbers. Surely the barons would rally to him against the invaders. But meanwhile, Conan himself lay helpless in a dark cell while others led his soldiers and fought for his crown. The king ground his powerful teeth in furious rage.
