As the ape came charging at him, swinging its massive arms wide, he dove between them and struck with all his desperate strength. He felt the blade sink to the hilt into the creature’s hairy chest, and instantly, letting go of it, he ducked his head and tightened his whole body into a compact knot of muscle. As he did, he grabbed the closing arms and drove his knee hard into the monster’s belly, bracing himself against the crushing grip.

For one dizzy moment he felt as if he were being torn apart by an earthquake; then suddenly he was free, sprawling on the floor, while the monster gasped out its life beneath him, its red eyes rolling upward, the hilt of the dagger quivering in its chest. His desperate strike had found its mark.

Conan was panting as if after a long fight, trembling in every limb. Some of his joints felt as though they had been wrenched out of place, and blood dripped from scratches where the creature’s claws had raked his skin; his muscles and tendons had been brutally strained and twisted. If the beast had lived a second longer, it would have torn him apart. But the Cimmerian’s immense strength had held out, for the brief instant it lasted, against the dying spasms of the ape that would have ripped a weaker man to pieces.

Conan bent down and pulled the knife from the monster’s chest. Then he quickly went up the stairs. He couldn’t guess what other horrors the darkness might hide, but he had no wish to meet any more. This kind of close, desperate fighting was exhausting even for the giant Cimmerian. The moonlight was fading from the floor, the darkness closing in, and something close to panic drove him up the stairway. He let out a gusty sigh of relief when he reached the top and felt the third key turn in the lock. He opened the door slightly and craned his neck to look through, half expecting an attack from some human or beastly foe.

He looked into a bare stone hallway, dimly lit, and a slender, graceful figure stood before the door.

“Your Majesty!” It was a low, vibrant cry, half relief and half fear. The girl rushed to his side, then hesitated, suddenly shy.

“You’re bleeding,” she said. “You’ve been hurt!”

He brushed off the concern with an impatient gesture.

“Just scratches that wouldn’t hurt a baby. Your little skewer came in handy, though. If not for it, Tarascus’ pet ape would be cracking my bones for the marrow right now. But what now?”

“Follow me,” she whispered. “I’ll lead you outside the city walls. I have a horse hidden there.”

She turned to lead the way down the corridor, but he placed a heavy hand on her bare shoulder.

“Walk beside me,” he said quietly, wrapping his massive arm around her lithe waist. “You’ve played fair with me so far, and I’m inclined to believe you; but I’ve survived this long only by never trusting anyone too much—man or woman. So! If you betray me, you won’t live to enjoy it.”

She didn’t flinch at the sight of the blood-stained dagger or the feel of his hard muscles pressing against her supple body.

“Strike me down without mercy if I betray you,” she said. “The very feel of your arm around me, even in threat, is like the fulfillment of a dream.”

The vaulted hallway ended at a door, which she opened. Outside lay another black man, a giant wearing a turban and silk loincloth, with a curved sword lying on the floor near his hand. He didn’t move.

“I drugged his wine,” she whispered, stepping carefully around the motionless figure. “He’s the last and outer guard of the pits. No one has ever escaped from them before, and no one has ever wanted to enter them; so only these black men guard them. Only they, of all the servants, knew it was King Conan that Xaltotun brought in his chariot as a prisoner. I was watching, sleepless, from an upper window that looked into the courtyard while the other girls slept; for I knew a battle was being fought, or had been fought, in the west, and I feared for you…

“I saw the blacks carry you up the stairs, and I recognized you in the torchlight. I slipped into this wing of the palace tonight, just in time to see them take you to the pits. I hadn’t dared come here before nightfall. You must have lain in a drugged sleep all day in Xaltotun’s chamber.

“Oh, we must be careful! Strange things are happening in the palace tonight. The slaves said Xaltotun sleeps as he often does, drugged by the lotus of Stygia, but Tarascus is in the palace. He came secretly, through the postern gate, wrapped in a cloak dusty from long travel, attended only by his squire, the lean, silent Arideus. I don’t understand it, but I’m afraid.”

They came to the foot of a narrow, winding stair, and climbing it, passed through a small panel that she slid aside. When they had gone through, she slipped it back into place, and it became just another part of the ornate wall. They were now in a larger corridor, carpeted and hung with tapestries, where golden light from hanging lamps glowed softly.

Conan listened intently, but heard no sound anywhere in the palace. He didn’t know what part of it he was in, or which direction led to Xaltotun’s chamber. The girl trembled as she drew him along the corridor, stopping beside an alcove hidden by satin drapes. Pulling these aside, she motioned for him to step into the niche and whispered, “Wait here! Beyond that door at the end of the corridor, we could meet slaves or eunuchs at any hour. I’ll go see if the way is clear before we risk it.”

At once his quick suspicions flared up.

“Are you leading me into a trap?”

Tears welled in her dark eyes. She dropped to her knees and clutched his strong hand.

“Oh, my king, don’t doubt me now!” Her voice trembled with desperate urgency. “If you hesitate, we’re doomed! Why would I bring you out of the pits just to betray you now?”

“All right,” he muttered. “I’ll trust you—though, by Crom, the habits of a lifetime aren’t easy to break. Still, I wouldn’t harm you now, even if you brought every swordsman in Nemedia down on me. If not for you, Tarascus’ cursed ape would have found me chained and unarmed. Do as you will, girl.”
